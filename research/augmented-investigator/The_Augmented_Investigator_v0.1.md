## Abstract

Large language models may expand the methodological reach of independent researchers by coupling human question formation with interactive computational assistance. This retrospective single-investigator case study examines the development of Architectural Economy in Biological and Technological Systems, an interdisciplinary manuscript that began with an inquiry into noncoding DNA and evolved into computational benchmarks and an exploratory genomic analysis. A working manuscript dated 23 November 2026 reported no original empirical findings; Version 7, dated 24 November, reported 16,384 multiplication verification checks, 540 exhaustive network comparisons and a genomic pilot comprising 636 regulatory regions across 408 genes. The pilot did not support its predicted association, and a sensitivity analysis weakened an initially favorable result. The manuscript distinguished this negative finding from subsequently proposed hypotheses about functional continuity. The observed artifacts support feasibility of a human-directed AI-assisted workflow but do not establish causal productivity gains, independent reproducibility or superiority over unaided investigators. We propose a trace-based framework for measuring scientific agility, methodological reach, human agency, error correction and evidential quality. The case motivates prospective comparisons and preservation of complete interaction and execution records.

**Keywords:** metascience; metacognition; human–AI collaboration; scientific agility; methodological reach; computational reproducibility; independent research.

## 1. Introduction

Scientific inquiry requires translating questions into operational definitions, locating evidence, choosing methods, implementing analyses, detecting errors and revising interpretations. The cost of acquiring these capabilities can prevent an interdisciplinary question from becoming empirical. Interactive AI may reduce this friction without removing the need for expertise and accountability. This paper studies the process that generated, tested and revised the genomic hypothesis, not the truth of the hypothesis itself. The investigator describes AI as enabling statistical and mathematical work he could not readily perform alone, emphasizing rapid interactive terminal exploration [1]. This is experienced capability, not a measured counterfactual.

## 2. Background and research questions

Metacognition concerns monitoring and regulating cognition [2]. Here the coupled process of human questions, computational assistance, code, evidence and interpretations is the object of study. Metascience provides a complementary framework. SciSciGPT demonstrates AI-supported research workflows and iterative prototyping [3]. A randomized quantitative social-science reproducibility study found AI-assisted teams did not outperform human-only teams overall, although they outperformed AI-led teams [4]. AI assistance must not be equated with scientific improvement. This case focuses on open-ended inquiry rather than standardized replication.

- RQ1: What changes in scope and maturity are documented across manuscript versions?
- RQ2: Through which observable interactions does AI appear to facilitate formalization, implementation, critique and revision?
- RQ3: How can evidential integrity be assessed independently of speed or sophistication?

## 3. Materials and methods

Retrospective descriptive N-of-1 case study with embedded artifact analysis. A later transcript study will use a research episode (question, method, execution/evaluation, decision) as the unit of analysis. Present evidence comprises selected artifacts and author statements, not a complete coded corpus. No unobserved contribution, timestamp or decision is imputed. Exploratory, not preregistered.

## 4. Results: documented trajectory

### 4.1 Expansion of analytical scope

V7 reports 30 seeded portfolios of combinatorial networks and a PDP-8 multiplication benchmark. At 32 synthetic outputs, sharing reduces distinct nodes by 50.9% while increasing mean motif-fault impact from 0.96 to 4.08; targeted buffering reduces the latter to 0.10. At 64 PDP-8 tasks, sharing saves 70.8% of code words while increasing execution cost and shared-fault exposure. V7 reports 16,384 multiplication checks and 540 comparisons across 4,096 input states without discrepancies [8]. Breadth of reported work is not itself proof of collaboration quality.

### 4.2 A negative genomic result as an epistemic test

H2 combines K562 perturbation profiles, ABC enhancer–gene mappings and 241-way phyloP. Of 695 original intervals, 694 uniquely mapped; 636 complete cases across 408 genes remained. Adjusted reach −0.026 (95% CI −0.094 to 0.042); interaction 0.012 (−0.056 to 0.080). Held-out chromosome MSE rises from 0.4332 (baseline) to 0.4356 (reach) and 0.4390 (full model) [8].

Within-gene buffering coefficient −0.398 (p=0.046) reverses to +0.704 (p=0.129) when focal raw ABC numerator is included. The manuscript rejects a robust interpretation. This episode is informative about evidential discipline, but neither the origin of the correction nor the necessity of AI is established.

### 4.3 Revision without retrospective confirmation

V7 separates unsupported H2 from prospective functional continuity (H3) and molecular functional equivalence (H3-M) tests. These are proposals, not findings.

## 5. Framework for studying augmented inquiry

Scientific agility is the capacity to move from a question to reviewable empirical results and revised questions while retaining validity and provenance. It is not synonymous with text output or computation count. Proposed measures: question-to-evidence latency; validated methodological reach; substantive iteration; human agency; epistemic integrity; research cost. No corpus-wide values are estimated here.

### 5.1 Proposed process model

Human question → joint operationalization → AI-assisted implementation → executed test → human/AI critique → revised question. Each transition retains prompt, method, code version, data identifier, output, interpretation and disposition. The model is proposed rather than observed in every episode.

### 5.2 Trace audit and prospective evaluation

Freeze relevant complete ChatGPT and Gemini/Antigravity transcripts, commits and artifacts. Hash and timestamp sources; preserve unfavorable outcomes and failed work; redact irrelevant personal material. Segment episodes using a documented codebook, double-code independently and report agreement. Independently rerun computational and genomic experiments, checking licenses, dependencies and results. Prospective human-only and AI-assisted matched tasks should use equivalent resources, blinded quality assessment, randomized or counterbalanced order where feasible, and error/correction-time recording. See [TRACE_AUDIT.md](TRACE_AUDIT.md).

## 6. Discussion and limitations

The observed sequence establishes bounded feasibility: an independent investigator working with multiple AI systems produced a versioned manuscript with computational experiments, a negative genomic pilot and prospective revisions. It does not establish causal amplification. No unaided control, complete process trace or independent replication exists for this draft. A rapid manuscript sequence is not proof of faster valid discovery.

Potential mechanisms include translation, lower implementation barriers, rapid alternatives and externalized reasoning. Risks include automation bias, fabricated references/outputs, data leakage, invalid proxies, confirmation bias and illusion of understanding. AI also assists this metascience paper; independent review and provenance guard against self-serving interpretation. A meaningful question may precede mastery of every implementation skill if the final method and assumptions are understood and verified. This educational proposition remains testable.

## 7. Conclusion

The first study investigates architectural economy; this study investigates the architecture of inquiry that produced it. V7 is both scientific manuscript and case artifact. Its negative pilot and explicit prospective hypotheses provide an initial basis for studying critical evaluation. The next step is an auditable dataset and tests of the hypothesized benefits and liabilities of augmentation.
