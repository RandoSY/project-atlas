# Idea Preservation Principle

**The Intellectual Estate is not a file backup. It is an idea backup.**

Files, repositories, screenshots, manuals, code, comics, notebooks, validation reports, and datasets are evidence containers. They matter because they help preserve and reconstruct the ideas. They are not the estate’s ultimate unit of value.

For every durable project or concept, preservation should make it possible for a future reader to recover:

1. **The problem** — what need, question, frustration, or opportunity motivated the work.
2. **The central idea** — the smallest clear statement of what the project contributes.
3. **Why it matters** — educational, scientific, engineering, practical, or human significance.
4. **How it works** — architecture, mechanisms, protocols, models, experiments, or design logic.
5. **Why it was designed that way** — important tradeoffs, rejected alternatives, constraints, and reasoning.
6. **What is distinctive** — the part that would be easy to lose if only implementation files survived.
7. **Evidence** — representative source, experiments, validation, measurements, manuals, figures, or demonstrations supporting the idea.
8. **Evolution** — meaningful changes in the idea over time, without preserving every duplicate version merely because it exists.
9. **Limits and unfinished work** — what was not validated, what remained unresolved, and what a future builder should test next.
10. **Reconstruction path** — enough context that another person, or an AI working with that person, could reproduce, extend, teach, or reinterpret the work.

## Recovery priority

When time or storage is limited, prioritize in this order:

1. unique ideas and design rationale;
2. canonical explanations and system architecture;
3. representative working source or executable artifacts;
4. validation evidence and experimental data;
5. teaching material and visual explanations, especially comics that communicate the idea efficiently;
6. historically meaningful versions that show conceptual evolution;
7. duplicate or mechanically generated files only when needed for provenance or reconstruction.

A project is not preserved merely because its files exist somewhere. It is preserved when its **meaning can survive the original author**.

## Estate test

For each project, ask:

> If the original working directories vanished and a capable future reader had only the preserved estate, could they understand what this was, why it existed, why it was important, how it worked, what evidence supported it, and how to carry it forward?

If the answer is no, the idea has not yet been adequately preserved.

This principle governs Project Atlas and all subsequent Intellectual Estate recovery work.
