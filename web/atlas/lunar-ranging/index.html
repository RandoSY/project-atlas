How far away is the Moon?

Send a pulse. Wait for the echo. Measure time. From a classroom Arduino to Apollo's mirrors, one deceptively simple idea opens the door to astonishing precision.

These are broad historical scales, not a homogeneous series of uncertainties. Modern observations measure station-to-reflector range, not a single fixed center-to-center distance.

Mirrors that never need batteries

Apollo 11 and 14 placed 100-cube arrays; Apollo 15 placed a 300-cube array. Soviet Lunokhod 1 and 2 also carried reflectors. Corner cubes return light toward its source. Only a tiny fraction of emitted photons return; researchers accumulate events and correct their measurements.

Results include lunar recession, tests of gravity, lunar interior constraints and Earth orientation. NASA Apache Point station.

The Moon is escaping — why?

Earth spins faster than the Moon orbits. Tidal dissipation offsets Earth's tidal bulge ahead of the Moon; its gravity transfers angular momentum to the Moon's orbit. The Earth slows, and the lunar orbit expands. Some mechanical energy becomes heat.

Build an echo-ranging instrument

Uno + HC-SR04 + ruler. The same equation, with sound instead of light. Use Demo first, then connect real hardware. No shield display dependency.
