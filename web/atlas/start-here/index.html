Start Here

A getting-started guide to Randall Young’s Project Atlas

Project Atlas is a public guide to experiments, classroom activities, instruments, and their source records. You do not need to understand the whole archive to use it. Pick one question, try one thing, and follow the evidence.

Download the two-page PDF

Make it your own

A friendly heads-up. Some Atlas software is ready to run, some is a prototype, and some projects are still designs. Find the GitHub repo and start with its README.

Give your AI of choice the repo link, your hardware, and what you want to do. Ask it to adapt the code or build what’s missing, then test one small task on your setup.

Seven places to begin

Try the first move before reading everything.

  • Motion

    The Singing Rule

    See the original ruler and Bluetooth setup, then compare measured ringdown with a model you can pluck.

    First move Shorten the virtual ruler and predict what its frequency will do.

  • Energy

    Pop-Can Calorimeter

    Follow candle mass loss and water heating. Learn why fuel power and heat captured by the water are different quantities.

    First move Switch between two heating curves with equal total water heat.

  • Measurement

    SQM+ Hallway Observatory

    Use an 11 m course to separate stopwatch speed, device recording, and experimental step detection.

    First move Change the candidate count in the calculator without changing the timed speed.

  • Physical computing

    Virtual Instruments

    See original dashboard captures and Pico W or ESP32 build paths for five wireless instruments.

    First move Pick a voltmeter, frequency meter, scale, ToF ruler, or thermometer.

  • Mathematics

    Ten Problems That Teach Calculus

    Start with a motion problem where total distance and net change are not the same thing.

    First move Try Problem 06, then use the worked solutions or handouts.

  • Curriculum

    CORE 10

    Connect the Kitchen Inquiry Lab, One Cup Chemistry, and the Personal Energy Laboratory.

    First move Compare the energy an appliance uses with the heat gained by a cup of water.

  • Time and accumulation

    Galileo’s Water Clock

    Calibrate a stream of water as an interval timer, then transfer it to a rolling-ball experiment.

    First move Compare steady and draining flow in the browser model.

Capture → Analyze → Publish

  1. Capture

    Write down what you observed and how you measured it.

  2. Analyze

    Compare it with a model, reference, or another run.

  3. Publish

    Save the method, evidence, result, and limits so someone else can repeat it.

Read the evidence

A measured run, a simulated example, and a working dashboard are different kinds of evidence. Read the captions and validation notes before you draw a conclusion. A good first experiment is one you can explain and repeat.
