02 / Experience before equation

Two instruments, two kinds of inquiry

A · Gas collection over water

Hold peroxide amount constant, vary yeast, compare controls and record gas volume over time. Use only small quantities and household 3% peroxide. Follow the teacher guide; do not obstruct the gas path or seal the system.

B · Balloon calibration

Introduce known air volumes, measure circumference and compare the spherical model with reality. The balloon is not connected to the peroxide reaction. It teaches calibration, geometry, nonlinearity and the chain rule.

Diagrams are instructional illustrations, not photographs of completed Atlas experiments. University of Minnesota Science for All classroom photos show related real experiments. Follow the Atlas safety protocol rather than copying the photographed equipment.

04 / The scientific bridge to C2G

What must persist? What can change? How much is enough?

Different PDP-8 hardware implementations can preserve specified instruction behavior. This motivates a biological question: can organisms preserve adequate peroxide-management capability despite changes in molecular implementation? The analogy generates hypotheses, not proof of a shared evolutionary mechanism.

Evidence layerWhat it establishes or tests
Kitchen experimentVisible reaction, apparent gas production and effects of yeast amount. It does not compare different molecular architectures.
Purified enzyme assaysVariability in catalytic performance and stability; distinct catalase families can perform the same reaction (Zamocky et al.).
Cellular perturbationComplementarity of catalases and alkyl hydroperoxide reductase in E. coli under different peroxide conditions (Seaver & Imlay).
Peroxide balanceProduction, removal, transport and exposure matter; adequate protection is not identical to complete removal (flux study).
Comparative evolutionProposed held-out tests of structural and systems predictors. No independent predictive validation or proof of ancient ancestry yet.

The kitchen experiment is the entry point. Published assays, open genomic databases and reproducible computation extend the same functional question, rather than treating the classroom experiment as proof of evolutionary conservation.
