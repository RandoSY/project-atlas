Does asking two people to make the same movement mean asking them to do the same physical work? Take a step-up. At the same cadence and step height, raising more body mass takes more external work against gravity. Matching that estimate still does not make the effort feel the same for both people.

01 · The step-test example

Prescribe, check, observe

  1. Prescribe a target. Define step rise, duration, the ascent event, and the beat pattern. The documented Android step protocol uses four beats per cycle: up, up, down, down. Body mass, step rise, and target watts determine the requested cadence.
  2. Check the motion. A phone can receive detected footfall events when the Android step detector is available and use acceleration as a separate motion check. Detected events are evidence, not proof of technique or the height of each ascent. The documented compliance calculation compares detected footfalls and cadence with the requested cue.
  3. Observe heart-rate response. A connected sensor supplies heart-rate readings during the effort. Heart rate is a separate observation, not a measurement of external watts or a guarantee that the effort is safe.

03 · Documented prototype walkthrough

Follow one test through the Android prototype

This walkthrough is adapted from the 23 August 2026 Android/Kotlin prototype notes. It describes the operating sequence and software logic for the dated prototype. The archived captures above document two observed outcomes, not a new validation or the behavior of a later build.

  1. Setup. The operator enters body mass, step height, target external power, duration, and a heart-rate ceiling specified by the protocol. The app calculates the target cadence and corresponding four-beat cue.
  2. Sensors and run. The operator selects a Bluetooth heart-rate sensor, checks for a live reading, and secures the phone before starting. During the test the app shows received heart rate and, on supported hardware, detected footfalls and observed cadence.
  3. Stop and interpret. In this documented version, a heart-rate reading at or above the entered ceiling, a sensor disconnect, or eight seconds without an update stops the software cues, timing, and recording and shows the reason. Manual stop is also available. It cannot physically stop a person. The notes do not establish a completion decision for missing motion in the current release candidate.
Measured signals
Phone acceleration and heart-rate sensor readings received by the app.
Derived values
Target and estimated motion cadence, the simple work model, and footfall detections. Neither calculated watts nor compliance is a direct sensor reading.
Software decisions
Compliance scoring and stop rules are interpretations of inputs and configured thresholds. They are not clinical judgments.

04 · Teach it or carry it forward

Change the question, change the evidence

Compare the two example prescriptions before touching hardware. Ask students which facts they would need to verify an effort, and what heart rate can tell them that cadence cannot. Agree on a protocol, how observations are to be checked, and how individual records will be kept private before any real session.

SQM+ asks how a person walked over a known course, starting from distance and time and testing what the motion trace adds. Fair Fitness Testing asks how a person responds to a prescribed effort and whether motion evidence supports that it was done. Fair Fitness Scout is a related branch for hallway mobility tests, not the Android step-test walkthrough shown here.
